# Human outreach playbook · drafts, not scheduled sends

**Before contact:** founders approve audience, jurisdictions, wording and each channel. Prefer an introduction or a volunteered address/reply. Check community rules and local electronic-marketing law; a public directory listing is not consent. Do not obtain private addresses, scrape, enrich, buy lists, or automate sends. No founder approval = prepare materials only.

1. **Qualify a firm, not a person's private life.** Public service page shows recurring client delivery; ask whether team is 5–30, fixed-fee/retainer mix, and whether delivery-to-billing handoff exists. Skip competitors' customer lists, vulnerable or regulated workloads, no-contact signals, and poor-fit teams.
2. **Minimal log:** organization/website, source/permission, role not demographics, stage, date, next agreed step, opt-out, anonymized pain and tools volunteered. Don't store private financial figures, credentials, customer names, payroll, screenshots or marketing lists in the product test DB. Review/discard stale leads after the approved period. Suppress further contact on opt-out.
3. **Introduction request to a known contact:** “I'm researching how small web studios hand a completed milestone to whoever invoices it. If a studio owner you know wants to discuss the process, would you pass them my note? No need to share their details.”
4. **Personal note after permission:** “Thanks for the introduction. Your studio describes [specific public service]. On the last project, how did the delivery lead tell finance it was ready to bill? We're testing one workflow with fictional data, not asking you to move your books. Would a 15-minute conversation help? No problem if not.” Personalize manually and truthfully. Don't fake familiarity, scarcity or savings.
5. **Discovery before demo (5 minutes):** last occurrence, who updated what, consequence if missed, current workaround, tool that must stay, who decides, sensitive data exclusions. Ask permission before taking notes/recording; default no recording. If no recent pain, finish without a pitch.
6. **Demo (7 minutes):** show `/demo` labeled fictional → fresh agency workspace → choose currency and import example client → project/deadline → teammate logs minutes → project-linked invoice and recorded payment → owner sees open balance; employee cannot view ledger. Ask the buyer to drive one step; don't silently “fix” their answers. Explain sample vs functioning DB, internal reminder vs client email, pricing proposal vs live charge.
7. **Close:** “What would prevent a second project next week? Which one screen or export is missing? Who would evaluate the proposed $29/$59 workspace fee?” Ask permission for one follow-up and preferred date. No contract, deposit or automatic invite.
8. **One follow-up, only if agreed:** “You mentioned [handoff]. Here is the short checklist you asked for. Would you like to try it with one colleague using sample data? If not, I'll close the loop.” Stop on opt-out, invalid contact, silence after agreed follow-up, or channel restriction. No drip sequence.

**Feedback card:** segment / first-hand recent behavior / workaround / pain in buyer's words (no identifying detail) / time or cost *if volunteered* / unaided task result / would return? / approver / stated price response vs actual action / blocker / next consented contact. Weekly: cluster by problem, not feature count; prioritize by frequency across distinct firms, severity, and implementation/support cost. Never turn interview comments into testimonials without explicit, separate permission.
