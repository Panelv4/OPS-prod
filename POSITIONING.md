# Positioning hypothesis

**For small web and creative agencies, OpsPilot connects a client's project to its invoice so the owner can see what was delivered, what was recorded as paid, and what still needs follow-up.** Use it alongside the accountant's books. The first test is whether this replaces a recurring handoff, not whether the team likes a new dashboard.

**Headline:** From client brief to outstanding balance. **Subhead:** Give delivery and finance a shared project record without giving everyone payroll or ledger access. **CTA:** Try the fictional walkthrough; no sign-in. Secondary CTA after operational approval: set up one engagement in an empty workspace.

Proof path, with only synthetic records: import a customer → agree budget/deadline → assign a task and log minutes → invoice that project → record a payment and an allocated expense → compare open balance/net recorded cash → inspect role-restricted view. Clearly distinguish a record of payment from moving money. Ask a deterministic question over these rows; show that the answer changes after a recorded event.

| Objection | Honest response |
|---|---|
| “We already use a board and our accountant has software.” | Keep the books. Which handoff, if any, is still manual? If there isn't one, don't migrate. |
| “Is this cheaper than Productive/Scoro?” | Compare the buyer's required tier, seats, implementation and term. Our workspace prices are proposals, not an existing paid offer or a parity claim. |
| “Will AI see client documents or send reminders to clients?” | Core reports use local permission-scoped queries. Only optional `help:` text goes to configured OpenAI. Reminders are internal; no autonomous external client contact. |
| “Can my clients sign in? Can it run payroll for us?” | No client portal. Payroll outputs are simplified estimates, not filings or bank disbursements. |

Prohibited copy: “fully compliant”, “guaranteed savings”, “verified by thousands”, “replaces your accountant”, “production certified”, “instant live payments”. Use no testimonials, customer logos or outcome percentages without genuine evidence, consent and review.
